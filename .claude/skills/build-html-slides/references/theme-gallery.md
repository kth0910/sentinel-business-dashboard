# Theme Gallery

Choose a primary visual system after identifying the audience, communication job, subject, authentic media, and emotional tone. The numbered headings below are the canonical named selector inventory from `../scripts/theme_registry.json`; names and order must match the registry exactly. Their visual grammar is optional vocabulary, not presets or a closed taxonomy of possible design systems. Write a bespoke, unnamed theme contract when none fits, but never invent a selector theme name or choose from the topic noun alone.

## 1. Swiss Signal

- Best for: strategy, public policy, research, architecture, serious proposals
- Type: neo-grotesk sans with a restrained serif or mono annotation role
- Palette: paper or black base, one signal red, one cool utility accent
- Grammar: strict grid, oversized numerals, thin rules, cropped documentary photography, asymmetric alignment
- Signature slides: thesis poster, data strip, annotated matrix, full-width timeline
- Avoid: tiny all-caps everywhere, sterile empty space with no evidence, safety-orange control-room palettes, and repeated incident-dashboard cards or status badges

## 2. Editorial Culture

- Best for: brand stories, portfolios, art, fashion, people, cultural topics
- Type: high-contrast serif display with clean sans body
- Palette: black, white, one saturated editorial color, occasional muted photo-derived tones
- Grammar: magazine margins, pull quotes, photo collage, caption systems, alternating quiet and loud spreads
- Signature slides: cover story, profile split, contact-sheet gallery, quote interlude
- Avoid: imitating a specific magazine masthead or covering images with long paragraphs

## 3. Cinematic Noir

- Best for: games, film, mystery, entertainment, narrative introductions
- Type: expressive display face with highly legible sans support
- Palette: near-black plus two subject-derived accents; use light surfaces only as deliberate resets
- Grammar: full-bleed art, controlled vignettes, spoiler-safe chapter cards, dramatic scale shifts, thin luminous lines
- Signature slides: key-visual cover, scene-led premise, mood montage, recommendation/trigger warning, cinematic closing
- Avoid: cropping title art, logos, faces, or edge-important key visuals

## 4. Mission Control

- Best for: infrastructure, AI systems, operations, engineering, security
- Type: sturdy sans with mono labels and numeric details
- Palette: charcoal, warm white, safety orange, mint/cyan status accents
- Grammar: topology maps, control boundaries, event timelines, operational tables, photographic chapter breaks
- Signature slides: system context, closed loop, failure choreography, authority ladder, deployment roadmap
- Avoid: neon decoration without hierarchy, one enormous unreadable architecture diagram, and treating control-room grammar as permission to repeat cards, panels, chips, or badges across the deck

## 5. Product Keynote

- Best for: devices, software, launches, sales, feature announcements
- Type: bold clean sans; product brand typography when licensed and appropriate
- Palette: brand-led neutrals with one or two functional accents
- Grammar: isolated product photography, short benefit claims, oversized metrics, feature reveals, decision-oriented closing
- Signature slides: logo/product cover, hero feature, proof metric, lineup, price or availability, lifestyle payoff
- Avoid: homemade logos, fake product renders, and feature-card walls

## 6. Data Newsroom

- Best for: market reports, investigations, performance reviews, policy evidence
- Type: editorial sans with tabular numerals and compact source labels
- Palette: warm white, ink, one highlight, semantic red/green used sparingly
- Grammar: headline-first charts, annotated deltas, source rails, map or timeline evidence, conclusion boxes, and sourced context photography when the market concerns physical products, facilities, people, or infrastructure
- Signature slides: finding headline, one-chart argument, subject-setting image, before/after, timeline, methodology, implications
- Avoid: dashboard density and charts without a stated takeaway

## 7. Destination Magazine

- Best for: consumer travel guides, city and regional recommendations, itineraries, food trips, seasonal travel, and tourism storytelling
- Type: confident magazine display sans or a contemporary serif/sans editorial contrast; choose the direction from the destination and audience rather than defaulting every trip to a nostalgic serif
- Palette: derive two or three roles from the destination, season, and photography; allow energetic coral, sky blue, vermilion, citrus, ocean, forest, or night colors instead of forcing dark green and off-white
- Grammar: cover-line hierarchy, dominant destination photography, editorial grids, full-bleed and inset photo alternation, itinerary strips, neighborhood guides, route maps, compact price/time metadata, and issue-like section openers
- Signature slides: magazine cover, destination opener, 24/48/72-hour itinerary, neighborhood or food lineup, route map, practical-info spread, final recommendation
- Avoid: copied mastheads, field-research specimen labels, fake taped notes, repetitive attraction cards, empty photo frames, generic travel icons in place of destination imagery, and treating a leisure guide like a research report

## 8. Field Notes

- Best for: expeditions, field research, science communication, environmental topics, observational education, and evidence-led case studies; use for travel only when the communication job is an expedition diary or first-person field record
- Type: humanist sans with serif captions or handwritten accent used sparingly
- Palette: off-white, dark green or navy, one warm field accent, true photo colors
- Grammar: specimen labels, map fragments, photo captions, taped-note accents, observation-to-insight flow
- Signature slides: place-setting cover, field observation, annotated image, route/process, lessons learned
- Avoid: fake paper clutter, beige-only palettes, decorative stickers with no informational role, and applying field-journal styling to an ordinary leisure guide

## 9. Playful Modular

## 20. Idol Editorial Index

## Selection rules

1. Choose based on all six selector axes—job, evidence, density, emotional register, surface family, and focal anchor—not the topic noun or favorite color. A named gallery direction is never mandatory.
2. Write a one-line theme contract covering mood, type roles, palette roles, shape language, media treatment, and motion.
3. Pick three to five signature composition families from the selected theme and add two contrasting families for rhythm.
4. Keep the system recognizable across dark, light, image-led, and data-led slides.
5. Keep the selected registry entry and its generated contract intact. If another grammar is materially better, rerun the selector; do not borrow an unselected named theme as a slide-level accent.
6. For finance and stablecoin decks, choose Ledger Trust when the communication job is trust, reserves, payment flow, settlement, or risk. Use Data Newsroom instead when the primary job is market evidence, and Mission Control when the primary job is infrastructure operations.
7. For travel decks, classify the communication job before selecting a theme. Use Destination Magazine for ordinary leisure guides and trip planning, Travel Editorial Collage for photographic regional journeys or travel-memory publications, Soft Kinetic or Museum Catalog for premium hospitality, Documentary Photo Essay for reportage or heritage testimony, and Field Notes only for expeditions or observation-led field records. Do not select Field Notes merely because the subject is travel.
8. For idol decks, use Idol Editorial Index only when the job is multi-subject orientation, comparison, chronology, or curation. Use Pop Culture Scrapbook for one act, campaign, comeback, fandom, or personality-led story when authentic imagery supports it. Route business or historical stories by their actual job.
9. For a series of related technical decks, do not choose Mission Control or another dark-console treatment by topic reflex. Technical Conference is the stage-first default candidate for engineering studies and talks; use Protocol Proof for cryptographic claims and verification. Exclude recent theme and cover families, and vary surface, title anchor, hero form, typography, and accent strategy together.
10. Read `theme-taxonomy.md` when the topic could support several directions. Route by communication job and evidence medium first, then use a named gallery direction as a starting contract.
11. After the selector result is locked, do not reinterpret one named direction through another direction's palette or component grammar. Rerun the selector when the direction changes.
12. Bright Learning is informal routing vocabulary for calm, step-led instruction; Playful Modular is the named selector theme for participatory exercises, quizzes, and modular learning. Do not use the two names interchangeably.
13. Editorial Field Guide is informal routing vocabulary, not a named selector theme. Use Field Notes only for first-person observation and field evidence; route interpretation and caveats by communication job and evidence medium.
14. Product Keynote supports a sequence of features, proof, lineup, and offer. Keynote Minimal Full-Bleed supports one dominant authentic artifact and one short message per beat. Do not use minimal full-bleed pacing to hide missing product evidence.
15. Clinical Clarity serves patients, care teams, clinical operations, and medical teaching. Scientific Atlas serves research evidence, specimens, mechanisms, calibrated scales, and uncertainty. Medicine as a topic noun does not decide between them.
16. Use selector-generated semantic color tokens only as paired foreground/background roles. Never override only the foreground or only the background member of a generated pair.
17. `Margin Commentary` and `Spatial Interface` are retired. `Systems Blueprint` is an invalid invented alias. Do not select, imitate, or reintroduce them.
18. Treat selector ranking as a candidate list. Use design judgment across subject, purpose, audience, language, evidence, and authentic assets; lock a non-primary candidate only through `--select-theme` with a concrete reason.
19. Sparse keynote pacing is not a universal quality signal. Except for deliberate hero, quote, vision, or chapter beats, avoid half-empty frames, large underfilled panels, and cover compositions whose title and visual anchor fail to control the canvas.
