# Human-Designed Reference Sources

Use this list only to study principles from credited designers and studios. Do not copy a composition, masthead, illustration, brand mark, or identity pixel for pixel. Record the useful principle in the design brief, then rebuild it from the deck's own subject, evidence, type system, and imagery.

## Travel editorial

- [Wandering Japan Leaflet Design — Aycan Yılmaz](https://www.behance.net/gallery/227098337/Wandering-Japan-Leaflet-Design): balance practical travel information with expressive, place-led visuals.
- [Tabi no Omoide — Yusuke Yamazaki](https://www.behance.net/gallery/114688617/Tabi-no-Omoide): use a warm contemporary system for travel memory, photography, notes, and keepsakes without fake vintage clutter.
- [VJ Korea, Japan National Tourism Organization — Yuko Furuya](https://www.behance.net/gallery/89140037/VJ-editorial-design): build an editorial destination system from authentic photography, publication hierarchy, and restrained Japanese cues.
- [Travel guide for Hokkaido — Y8N](https://www.behance.net/gallery/79796351/Travel-guide-for-Hokkaido): combine photo-book pacing, maps, and useful regional information.

Derived rule: use one dominant place image, one or two subordinate detail crops, real route or editorial metadata, and a language-appropriate serif/sans contrast. Japan is not a license for generic dark green, fake calligraphy, red-sun decoration, or ornamental kanji.

## Technical conference and technology identity

- [BI Show — Tech Conference Branding — Sasha Prudskaya](https://www.behance.net/gallery/235037743/BI-Show-Tech-Conference-Branding-Visual-Identity): modular event identity with clear compositional logic across presentations, print, and digital surfaces.
- [WaysConf — Jacek Janiczak](https://www.behance.net/gallery/246318785/WaysConf-brand-identity): conference identity that remains recognizable across stage, schedule, and promotional media.
- [Go Cloud — Xenia Sennikova](https://www.behance.net/gallery/213129913/Go-Cloud-IT-Event-Logo-Key-Visual-Design): a technical key visual can feel energetic without becoming a fake dashboard.
- [Knotty Objects — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/knotty-objects/story): use a distinctive grid and typographic signal consistently across an MIT summit's stage, presentation, motion, and environment.
- [Platform — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/platform?page=1&query=conference&rel=search): strong black/white structure and bold color can form a flexible conference system.
- [Resonate — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/resonate?page=2&query=education&rel=search): grid, type, and glyph behavior create event identity without relying on decorative software chrome.

Derived rule: technical conference decks need a stage-readable title, literal system or artifact anchor, session metadata, and a flexible event signal. Do not default to console black, orbit diagrams, status cards, or model badges.

## Reports, evidence, and technical editorial

- [IEEE Spectrum — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/ieee-spectrum?page=1&query=conference&rel=search): explain complex technology with accessible editorial hierarchy, modular typography, and clear diagrams.
- [Lloyd's of London Annual Report — Pentagram](https://prod.origin.pentagram.com/work/lloyds-of-london): keep facts central with a lean, authoritative, usable publication system.
- [Sustainability Solutions Group — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/sustainability-solutions-group): let typography, data visualization, and editorial structure carry reports rather than filling pages with containers.
- [Graphcore — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/graphcore): balance technical product rigor with a distinctive editorial identity and keynote system.
- [Cohere — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/cohere?page=1&query=conference&rel=search): assign separate headline, reading, and mono roles to make a flexible technical system.

Derived rule: a report cover should state the finding, decision, or publication subject and show one factual anchor. Inside, use a compact source/method band, conclusion-led charts, and publication rhythm rather than a corporate card wall.

## Pop culture, music, and fan editorial

- [JAPJJI-CDC — Camille](https://www.behance.net/gallery/248115747/JAPJJI-CDC): contemporary K-pop magazine structure and expressive type/image pacing.
- [Idol Magazine Project — Georgeann McLemore](https://www.behance.net/gallery/167744515/Idol-Magazine-Project): editorial hierarchy for an idol-focused publication.
- [NEformat — Elena Kuzjaeva](https://www.behance.net/gallery/123013647/fanzin-neformat): a modular grid and stable header/footer can support a freer, rebellious fanzine body.
- [BEATBOX Mini Zine — Sanjana Kumar](https://www.behance.net/gallery/214016747/BEATBOX-Mini-Zine-%28NCT-Dream%29): use compact zine scale, photography, and fan-oriented sequencing.
- [NewJeans — How Sweet Zine — Anggito Priyambodo](https://www.behance.net/gallery/204212335/NewJeans-How-Sweet-Zine): combine authentic promotional imagery with a specific campaign language.

Derived rule: use authentic artist imagery, one campaign field, one dominant photo frame, a bold title, credits, and at most one supporting hand-drawn mark. A scrapbook is a paced editorial system, not random stickers, tape, and equal-weight photo tiles.

## Typography references

- [Typewolf Editor](https://www.typewolf.com/editor): study editorial display/readability contrast and type pairing; verify licensing and Korean/Japanese coverage separately.
- [The Meteor — Pentagram](https://www.pentagram.com/work/the-meteor/story): separate display, editorial, and corporate reading roles instead of forcing one face to do everything.

## Reference review

When the task benefits from external visual research:

1. Prefer credited designer, studio, publisher, conference, or brand case studies.
2. Inspect at least three relevant works and record only reusable principles: surface, title anchor, hero form, type roles, density, image behavior, and metadata.
3. Exclude AI-generated galleries, prompt collections, anonymous template farms, and unattributed moodboards.
4. Choose the final registered theme with judgment across subject, purpose, audience, evidence, language, and available authentic assets. The selector ranking is a candidate list, not an order that replaces design judgment.
5. Cite the references in the private design brief or `sources.json` only when their work materially influenced the deck.
